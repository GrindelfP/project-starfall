# Flight controls and tuning

The default `sandbox` scene starts in chase view. Right-click captures the mouse; in chase view relative mouse movement controls yaw and pitch (right turns right, up pitches up). Escape releases capture. W/S apply forward/reverse thrust; reverse thrust can brake forward motion, but continues into reverse if held. A/D yaw; Up/Down pitch; Space/Control apply vertical thrust in world space; Shift doubles thrust acceleration. Releasing thrust leaves velocity unchanged. C switches to the free camera and pauses ship simulation until chase view resumes; in free view W/A/S/D, Q/E, Shift and captured mouse retain the bindings in camera.md. R resets both poses and ship velocity. Switching camera modes or resetting discards queued mouse movement. Focus loss and minimization clear input and reset the frame timer.

The ship has world-space velocity and accelerates at 3 world units/s², or 6 with boost. Combined thrust and vertical input are normalized when needed. Each step adds acceleration × time to velocity, limits its magnitude artificially to 12 units/s, and advances position using the average of the old and new velocities. There is no drag or automatic braking; turning changes the thrust direction but does not rotate existing velocity. The speed limit introduces a small timestep-dependent approximation near the limit. Keyboard yaw/pitch rate is 1.4 radians/s. Mouse sensitivity is `Ship::mouseSensitivity` (0.0025 radians per raw count); pitch is clamped to ±(π/2 − 0.05) radians. Mouse displacement is applied directly, without a frame-time multiplier. The chase eye follows instantly, 7 units behind the ship's forward direction and 2.5 units above it, looking at the ship with world up. Ship cube parts share its orientation. Asteroid contacts sweep the ship's translated shapes between previous and current positions and reset the ship, including velocity; the sweep uses the final orientation and does not cover rotation during the step. There is no obstacle avoidance.
`--scene reference` retains the original reference cubes and free camera for renderer comparisons. `--scene sandbox --demo --frames 120` runs a repeatable scripted turn using fixed 1/60-second simulation steps for bounded smoke runs; normal flight uses the clamped frame timer. `--backend dx11|dx12` selects the renderer.

Automated ship tests cover acceleration, coasting, turning while drifting, reverse braking, boost, speed limit, normalized thrust, turn rates, mouse direction, sensitivity, frame-count independence and pitch limits, part orientation, chase target, and reset. Interactive controls and visual readability require manual validation on a desktop.

Debug and Release configured and built; CTest passed 4/4 in each. Bounded sandbox demo smoke runs presented 30 frames and exited successfully in both configurations on both DX11 and DX12. These runs do not establish visual quality or interactive input behavior.
